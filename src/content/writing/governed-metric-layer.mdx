## The problem

Five spreadsheets, five definitions of contribution margin. Finance, Sales, Procurement, and Ops were each reasoning from a different version of the same number. Decisions that looked rigorous in any one meeting fell apart the moment two teams compared notes.

## The approach

The dbt semantic layer became the single governed definition. Power BI, Snowflake Intelligence, and ad-hoc SQL all consumed the same metric, not because the tools were aligned by default, but because the layer underneath gave them one trusted route.

## The tradeoff

Centralising metrics removes local flexibility. A team that wants to tweak rebate logic for one report can no longer quietly do it in a sidebar spreadsheet. That friction is a feature: it forces the conversation about what the metric means before anyone ships a number.

## What changed

The practical outcome is not a prettier model. It is a shared operating layer. When a commercial question moves from spreadsheet debate into governed metrics, the organisation spends less energy reconciling definitions and more energy deciding what to do.

That is why semantic layers matter. They are not just a modelling convenience. They are the place where data engineering, finance logic, commercial judgement, and AI-assisted analytics can meet without each interface inventing its own truth.
